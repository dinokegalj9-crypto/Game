> faction quest threads ([`Systems/QuestDesign.md`](Systems/QuestDesign.md) §12), the corruption

## 1. Philosophy — no good factions, no evil factions

There are **no good factions and no evil factions** in Aldermark — only people trying to
survive the end of the world by incompatible logics. **Every faction believes it is right.
Every faction has real strengths. Every faction has real flaws and a buried sin.** Conflict
emerges from **incompatible goals**, never cartoon villainy.

Each faction is driven by the same six hungers, weighted differently: **survival, influence,
power, security, meaning, legacy.** A faction the player finds monstrous is pursuing one of
these by means another faction finds monstrous in turn. The design's job is to make the player
**unable to find a clean side** — and to keep asking, long after the credits, *"did I choose
the right one?"* with no definitive answer ever given.

**The grey rule (every major faction passes all four):** they are *right* about something
important; they are *wrong* about something important; they have a strength that makes them
worth allying with; they have a sin that makes the alliance cost the player something.

---

## 2. Player Experience Goals

The political layer must make the player: **question loyalties · doubt information · change
opinions · feel political pressure · encounter moral ambiguity · never know the full truth.**
Information in this world is *always partisan* — every source has an agenda (§12). The player
acts on incomplete, contradictory testimony and lives with the consequences. Certainty is a
thing other people sell; the game never grants it.

---

## 3. The Fractured Kingdom — power before and after the Fall

**Before (the Old Order):** Aldermark was a feudal monarchy. The **King** ruled from the
capital; the **Church of the Last Dawn** held spiritual authority and literacy; **noble
houses** governed regions and fielded levies; **guilds and merchant houses** moved goods along
royal roads and rivers. Order was imperfect but whole.

**The Fall:** the King unearthed and wore the Hollow Crown. The sky began to die; the kingdom's
**twelve greatest figures — rulers, nobles, generals, saints — were corrupted into the Twelve
Lords** (`BossBible.md`). The King vanished. Central authority collapsed: heirs were corrupted
or killed, the roads grew unsafe, regions were cut off. **The factions of today are the
*survivors* of that collapse** — the remnants of those same houses and orders, now warring over
a corpse-kingdom.

**Who controls what, now** (power is fractured and contested — no one holds it all):

| Lever | Held (contested) by |
|-------|---------------------|
| **Religion / public opinion** | The Church of the Last Dawn (dominant), contested by the Silent Clergy and the Crown-Touched Cult |
| **Military / legitimacy** | The Crownsworn (the capital remnant), with noble levies and free companies |
| **Trade / money / roads** | The Ashbourne Houses (and the merchant guilds beneath them) |
| **Ports / rivers / smuggling** | The Drowned Brotherhood |
| **Wilds / game / food from the land** | The Blackwood Hunters; farming villages under noble protection |
| **Knowledge / records / literacy** | The Church (orthodox record) and the Lantern Order (the lost record) |
| **Communication** | Whoever holds the roads and rivers — so, contested daily |
| **Resources / mining** | Local lords and the Houses; some sites lost to corruption |
| **The corruption itself** | The Crown-Touched Cult claims it; everyone else fears or denies it |

The map of power **shifts over a playthrough** as factions war (§15) and the player intervenes.

## 4. Religion — The Church of the Last Dawn

The dominant faith — ancient, literate, and the only institution to survive the Fall mostly
intact. It is why most people get up in the morning.

- **Core belief:** there was a **First Dawn** when the sun was whole; the world has fallen into
  the **Long Dusk** as punishment/test; if the faithful **endure** in rite and virtue, the
  **Last Dawn** will break and the sun be restored. Suffering is purification; the Crown's
  corruption is the Dusk deepening, to be *endured and resisted*, never used.
- **Sacred texts:** *The Book of Dawns* (canon, hopeful) and the older, restricted *Dusk Codex*
  (apocalyptic, read only by the high clergy).
- **Saints & martyrs:** a calendar of saints who endured great suffering — including, with
  terrible irony, **Saint Eleanor**, venerated as the saint who held Ashbourne in faith, *whom
  the Church does not admit is a corrupted Lord* (`BossBible.md` V).
- **Holidays / rites:** the **Long Vigil** (the year's darkest night, kept awake in prayer for
  the Dawn), the **Kindling** (relighting of every hearth and candle), funeral rites that matter
  intensely in a world where the dead don't rest.
- **Pilgrimages / sacred sites:** the great cathedrals; pilgrimage routes between them (a real
  driver of travel and encounters); the **Silent Cathedral** (contested with the schism).
- **Forbidden practices:** speaking the Crown's true name; using corruption-granted power;
  reading the Dusk Codex unsanctioned; necromancy of the unquiet dead.
- **Why people follow:** charity (the Church feeds and shelters), structure, literacy, funeral
  rites, and **hope** — the one institution offering a future.
- **Its strength:** organization, legitimacy, literacy, charity, reach into every settlement.
- **Its flaw & buried sin:** dogma that suppresses inconvenient truth — and the **hidden truth**
  that the Church's own past leadership *knew what the Crown was* (a churchman was among those
  who unearthed it) and **covered it up to preserve order**, canonizing the very people who were
  becoming Lords. The Church is not lying for power alone; it lies because it believes the truth
  would end all hope. That is its tragedy and its crime.

## 5. Heresies & Schisms

The faith has fractured under the Dusk. Each heresy is **partially correct**, and some hold
**hidden truths** the orthodox Church denies — the player uncovers which by play (§12).

| Heresy / schism | Claims | The grain of truth |
|-----------------|--------|--------------------|
| **The Silent Doctrine** (Silent Clergy) | The divine is reached through *silence*; speech is sin; the dark between the stars is real and words *call it* | Closest to the cosmic truth — reality is unraveling and *something* listens |
| **The Crown-Touched Creed** (the Cult) | The corruption is not punishment but **ascension** — *"the world is not dying; it is becoming something else"* | Right that the world is *becoming something* — catastrophically wrong that it is salvation |
| **The Dawnless Heresy** | The sun is *dead*; there will be no Last Dawn; the Church sells false hope to keep the desperate docile | Right that no natural dawn is coming — a despair the Church cannot refute |
| **The Drowned Heresy** (Brotherhood) | Salvation is *beneath* the water, in the old order the sea preserved, not above in a sun that won't return | Right that the surface order is finished |
| **Restorationism** (Crownsworn) | Faith belongs to the rightful **King**, not a dying sun; restore the throne and the Dusk lifts | Right that the throne's vacancy is a wound — wrong that filling it cures the rot |

The Church names all of these heresy and persecutes them; the player will meet sincere,
intelligent believers on every side.

## 6. The Political System

- **Nobility & inheritance:** feudal, patrilineal-with-exceptions; but the **inheritance system
  is broken** — too many heirs are dead, corrupted (the Lords were nobles), or contested. Claims
  now rest on *force, legitimacy, and Church sanction* as much as blood.
- **Authority is split** four ways and no longer reconciled by a King: **regional** (noble
  houses), **military** (the Crownsworn + house levies + free companies), **religious** (the
  Church, contested by schisms), **trade** (the Houses/guilds). Each claims primacy.
- **Law & justice:** local and harsh; a lord's word, a magistrate's ledger (House Voss / the Ash
  Magistrate, `BossBible.md` VI), or a Church court — and they often contradict. Justice is
  *whoever is strong enough to enforce a verdict.*
- **Corruption (political):** rife — bought verdicts, sold grain, purchased levies. The desperate
  are exploited everywhere; integrity is a luxury few afford.
- **Power struggles:** the empty throne is the great prize — the **Crownsworn** guard it for a
  King who won't return, the **Ashbourne Houses** maneuver to seat one of their own, the **Cult**
  wants no throne but the Crown itself, and the heir (**the Gilded Son**) is already lost to it.

## 7. Noble Houses

The great families that survived the Fall, now the secular power (mostly seated in or allied to
Ashbourne). *Fields: history · symbol · motto · territory · goals · enemies · secrets ·
reputation · scandals · resources.*

- **House Aldermark** (the royal line). *Symbol:* the rayed sun, now broken. *Motto:* "Until the
  Dawn." *Territory:* the Hollow Capital (held in trust). *History:* the King's own house; he
  wore the Crown and vanished; **Prince Aldous** (the **Gilded Son**, `BossBible.md` XII) is the
  lost heir. *Goal (its remnant's):* survive its own extinction. *Secret:* the King may yet
  live, changed. *Reputation:* sacred and doomed. *Resources:* legitimacy itself — the thing
  every other house covets.
- **House Vane.** *Symbol:* an iron gauntlet. *Motto:* "The Hand Holds." *Territory:* the Capital
  garrison. *History:* stewards and generals; **Regent Halis Vane** (the **Iron Regent**,
  `BossBible.md` XI) rules the empty throne. *Goal:* hold the realm together by force until the
  King returns. *Enemies:* the Ashbourne Houses (who want the throne for themselves). *Secret:*
  Vane took a fragment and is corrupting. *Reputation:* grimly respected. *Resources:* the only
  disciplined standing army left.
- **House Voss.** *Symbol:* a balanced scale over ash. *Motto:* "Order is Mercy." *Territory:*
  Ashbourne's courts. *History:* magistrates and lawkeepers; **Magistrate Voss** (the **Ash
  Magistrate**, `BossBible.md` VI) kept the law as the ash fell. *Goal:* preserve order at any
  cost. *Scandal:* enforced curfew-executions long past reason. *Secret:* Voss's ledger hides his
  own family's unfinished death-entry. *Resources:* law, records, the machinery of governance.
- **House Carrow.** *Symbol:* a laden scale-beam (merchant balance). *Motto:* "All Things Have a
  Price." *Territory:* Ashbourne's markets and the trade roads. *History:* merchant-nobles, new
  money married into old. *Goal:* profit and a throne to protect it. *Enemies:* the Drowned
  Brotherhood (who tax their river trade in blood). *Scandals:* grain-hoarding during famine;
  funding free companies on both sides of a dispute. *Resources:* money, roads, the guilds,
  mercenary contracts — the deepest purse in Aldermark.

The **Ashbourne Houses** faction (§10) is the council these families form — and feud within.

## 8. Economic Power

A world this broken runs on who controls scarcity:

| Lever | Controlled (contested) by |
|-------|---------------------------|
| **Food** | Blackwood Hunters (game) + farming villages under House protection; famine is constant leverage |
| **Trade & roads** | House Carrow / the Ashbourne Houses and the merchant guilds |
| **Rivers, ports, smuggling** | The Drowned Brotherhood (they tax or sink what crosses water) |
| **Crafting** | Settlement guilds and blacksmiths (the player's own settlement competes here) |
| **Mining / rare resources** | Local lords; several richest sites are *lost to corruption* — a prize and a death-trap |
| **Money flow** | House Carrow's coin and credit; the Church's tithes and charity |
| **Influence networks** | The Church (every parish) and the Cult (every shadow) — the two widest webs |

**Economic warfare is real warfare here:** cutting a road, hoarding grain, or sinking a barge
decides more than most battles (§15).

## 9. The Faction Framework

Every major faction is authored against fifteen fields: **Name · History/Founding · Beliefs ·
Leadership · Internal Structure · Goals · Resources · Enemies · Allies · Weaknesses · Secrets ·
Public Reputation · Actual Reality · Long-Term Plans.** The split between **Public Reputation**
and **Actual Reality** is mandatory — *what a faction is believed to be is never quite what it
is*, and the gap is where the player's disillusionment (and the propaganda system, §12) lives.

## 10. The Major Factions

### The Church of the Last Dawn
*Religion, public opinion, literacy.* — **Founding:** ancient, predating the kingdom; it crowned
the first Aldermark kings. **Beliefs:** §4. **Leadership:** a High Luminary and a synod of
bishops (riven by the schisms). **Structure:** parishes → dioceses → the synod; a hidden inner
order (the **Keepers of the Dusk Codex**). **Goals:** hold the faithful to endurance; suppress
the heresies; outlast the Dusk. **Resources:** reach, literacy, charity, tithes, moral
authority. **Enemies:** the Cult (its mirror-heresy), the Silent Clergy (its schism), the
Dawnless. **Allies:** of convenience with the Ashbourne Houses and Crownsworn (order serves
faith). **Weaknesses:** dogma, slowness, the rot of its own cover-up. **Secret:** it knows what
the Crown is and hid it (§4). **Public reputation:** humanity's shepherd and last comfort.
**Actual reality:** a frightened institution lying for what it believes is everyone's good.
**Long-term plan:** endure, and quietly contain the truth forever. **Corruption stance:**
*destroy* it (and bury that they could have warned the world).

### The Blackwood Hunters
*The wilds, game, monster-culling.* — **Founding:** a folk-order of woodsfolk and beast-slayers,
older than the Church's reach into the forest. **Beliefs:** nature must be *balanced*; the
corrupted must be culled before they spread; sentiment kills. **Leadership:** a council of Master
Hunters (no single head); the **Black Stag** and the **Greenfather** (`BossBible.md` III, IV) are
their fallen legends — revered *and* now hunted. **Structure:** lodges, master-and-apprentice
lines. **Goals:** contain the corruption at its wild edges; keep the Blackwood. **Resources:**
unmatched skill against the corrupted, game/food, woodcraft, the region itself. **Enemies:** the
Cult (spreaders of what they cull), House Carrow (who'd log their forest). **Allies:** wary, with
villages they protect. **Weaknesses:** insular, distrustful of outsiders (and the player),
under-manned. **Secret:** they sometimes burn whole infected villages to contain spread — and
call it mercy. **Public reputation:** grim heroes, or murderers, depending who you ask.
**Actual reality:** pragmatists carrying an unbearable triage. **Long-term plan:** hold the line
even if the kingdom is lost. **Corruption stance:** *destroy/contain* it — ruthlessly.

### The Ashbourne Houses
*Trade, money, secular governance.* — **Founding:** the surviving noble council seated in
ash-buried Ashbourne. **Beliefs:** civilization, bloodline legitimacy, and *order* must be
preserved; someone must rule, so let it be the worthy (themselves). **Leadership:** a fractious
council (Houses Voss, Carrow, and lesser families). **Structure:** feuding houses, guilds,
contracted free companies. **Goals:** restore central authority — under their hand; protect their
wealth. **Resources:** money, roads, administration, mercenaries (§7–8). **Enemies:** the
Crownsworn (rival claim), the Drowned Brotherhood (river tolls). **Allies:** the Church (order),
each other (barely). **Weaknesses:** infighting, decadence, self-interest, reliance on bought
swords. **Secret:** several houses *fund the Cult* quietly, hedging in case the corruption wins.
**Public reputation:** the last bastion of civilization. **Actual reality:** a nest of rivals
exploiting the desperate while the world ends. **Long-term plan:** seat an Ashbourne-controlled
monarch. **Corruption stance:** *fear and deny* it publicly; *profit from* it privately.

### The Drowned Brotherhood
*Rivers, ports, the deep.* — **Founding:** the **Drowned King's** court (`BossBible.md` VII),
continued in exile and in death by those who would not leave the water; the **Pearl Queen**
(VIII) is their sorrowing patron. **Beliefs:** the surface and its dying sun are finished;
the old order endures *beneath*, and loyalty to the drowned crown is salvation. **Leadership:**
tide-captains who claim the King's mandate. **Structure:** ship-clans, smuggler crews,
drowned-rite cults. **Goals:** control all water; gather the faithful below; outlast the surface.
**Resources:** ships, ports, smuggling, the Drowned Kingdom, fearlessness. **Enemies:** House
Carrow (trade), the Crownsworn (a rival "true crown"). **Allies:** none trusted — they deal,
they don't ally. **Weaknesses:** fanatical loyalty to a dead king; nihilism; landlocked
impotence. **Secret:** the King they serve *no longer knows them* (VIII's tragedy) — some
captains know, and lie to the rest. **Public reputation:** pirates and drowned madmen.
**Actual reality:** grief-stricken loyalists who chose the water over watching the sun die.
**Long-term plan:** when the surface falls, inherit what floats. **Corruption stance:**
*indifferent* — they expect the end regardless.

### The Silent Clergy
*The Silent Cathedral, esoteric truth.* — **Founding:** a schism (or an older root) of the
Church that took the dark seriously; keepers of the silence-law. **Beliefs:** the divine and the
abyss are reached through **silence**; speech is sin and *summons*; only the quiet endure
(§5). **Leadership:** the **Last Confessor** (`BossBible.md` X) and a hierarchy of the mute; the
**Choir of Thorns** (IX) is their broken sin. **Structure:** orders of silence, confessors,
wardens of sound. **Goals:** keep the silence that holds the horror back; guard what they know.
**Resources:** the Cathedral, discipline, esoteric knowledge (**they know the most about the
Crown's cosmic nature**), resistance to certain horrors. **Enemies:** the orthodox Church
(heresy), anything that makes noise. **Allies:** none — they barely speak. **Weaknesses:**
extremism (they kill the loud), paralysis, the truth they refuse to share could save lives.
**Secret:** their silence isn't only holy discipline — *the Choir proved that sound, here, truly
kills*, and they've hidden how close the unraveling is. **Public reputation:** terrifying
zealots. **Actual reality:** the people most right about the cosmic horror, ruined by being
right. **Long-term plan:** hold the silence until the end, and meet it quietly. **Corruption
stance:** *resist* it as the Dusk made manifest.

### The Crown-Touched Cult
*The corruption, hidden cells everywhere.* — **Founding:** sprang from the Fall itself, the first
to call the corruption *holy*. **Beliefs:** the Hollow Crown is the next stage of being —
*"the world is not dying; it is becoming something else"* (the kingdom's tagline is **their
creed**); the corrupted are blessed, the Lords are prophets, the player (if corrupting) is a
coming god. **Leadership:** a hierarchy by **corruption stage** — the most corrupted lead.
**Structure:** secret cells in every settlement and faction; no single visible head. **Goals:**
hasten the world's "becoming"; gather and elevate the corrupted; claim the Crown's fragments.
**Resources:** growth, fanatical devotion, corruption-granted power, eyes everywhere, hidden
House funding (§Ashbourne secret). **Enemies:** everyone who fears the rot — openly. **Allies:**
the corrupting, the desperate, the secretly-hedging powerful. **Weaknesses:** their salvation is
monstrous; internal stage-rivalry; they accelerate the apocalypse they welcome. **Secret:** even
they don't understand what the Crown truly *is* (the ancients' artifact) — their faith is
**sincere and blind**. **Public reputation:** monsters, denied or hunted. **Actual reality:**
true believers in transcendence, which is real and is annihilation. **Long-term plan:** the
world's complete "becoming." **Corruption stance:** *worship* it — and they will welcome a
corrupted player as no one else will.

### The Crownsworn
*Military, legitimacy, the empty throne.* — **Founding:** the King's own guard and loyal
generals, who never accepted his disappearance. **Beliefs:** the rightful King will return (or
must be restored); only legitimate monarchy ends the chaos; the Crown's horror is the *enemy*,
not a tool or a god. **Leadership:** the **Iron Regent, Halis Vane** (`BossBible.md` XI), ruling
in trust. **Structure:** a disciplined standing army, the Capital garrison, House Vane's levies.
**Goals:** hold the realm, restore the throne, destroy the corruption. **Resources:** the only
real army, the Capital's defenses, the claim of legitimacy. **Enemies:** the Ashbourne Houses
(rival claim), the Cult, the Drowned Brotherhood. **Allies:** the Church (order and legitimacy
serve each other). **Weaknesses:** they guard an **empty throne for a dead man**; rigidity;
Vane's own corruption. **Secret:** Vane knows the King will not return — and guards the throne
anyway, because the lie holds the realm together (and because he, too, has taken a fragment).
**Public reputation:** the last loyal defenders. **Actual reality:** devotion calcified into a
beautiful, doomed lie. **Long-term plan:** restoration — of a thing that cannot be restored.
**Corruption stance:** *destroy* it — while their own Regent succumbs.

## 11. Minor Powers

- **The Lantern Order** — a small, hunted order of scholars and archivists preserving the
  **lost history** (§12) the Church buried and the Cult would twist. Neutral-ish, fragile,
  invaluable to a player chasing the truth; the keepers of mysteries (`QuestDesign.md` §16).
- **The Free Companies** — mercenary bands selling steel to any house; loyal to coin, a
  destabilizing wildcard the Ashbourne Houses rent.
- **Folk-cults & village faiths** — local syncretic beliefs (the bell-folk of the Moors, the
  stag-rites of the Blackwood) that bend orthodox faith to regional dread; seedbeds of heresy
  and emergent story.

## 12. Propaganda & the Five Histories

**There is no neutral history in Aldermark.** Every faction tells a different past, and the
player uncovers the truth slowly, partially, and never completely (`QuestDesign.md` mystery
framework §16). Five strata of history exist:

| History | Held by | Says |
|---------|---------|------|
| **Official** | The Church / the Houses | The King was holy; the Dusk is a test; endure and the Dawn comes. |
| **Hidden** | The Church's inner order | The Church *knew* the Crown's nature and covered up its discovery (§4). |
| **Forbidden** | The Crown-Touched Cult | The Crown is ascension; the Lords are prophets; this is becoming, not dying. |
| **Lost** | The Lantern Order (fragmentary) | A **pre-kingdom civilization** *made* the Crown and vanished using it — the true, oldest layer. |
| **Manipulated** | Every faction, about itself | Each faction's self-serving edit (the Drowned King "chose" the sea; Saint Eleanor "ascended"). |

The player assembles the truth from biased testimony, suppressed records, and the world's own
strata (Art Bible "history in layers"). **The reward of digging is disillusionment** — learning
that no faction's story, including the one the player believed, is whole.

## 13. Espionage

Politics here is fought in shadow as much as in the open. Systems: **spies, informants, secret
networks, assassination, political manipulation, blackmail, sabotage.** Each faction excels
differently:

- **Cult** — the widest secret network (cells everywhere; they hear what's whispered to the corrupted).
- **Silent Clergy** — they *hear everything* (silence makes listeners); poor at acting on it.
- **Ashbourne Houses** — political spies, blackmail, bought verdicts, free-company sabotage.
- **Crownsworn** — disciplined military intelligence; weak at subtlety.
- **Drowned Brotherhood** — smuggling routes double as an information underground.

The player can be **recruited as an agent**, run their own informants, be *targeted* (an
assassination attempt is a consequence of standing), or have their crimes used against them
(blackmail — tying to the Reputation "witnesses/gossip" rules, `LivingWorld.md` §15).

## 14. Faction Reputation

Reputation is **per-faction and partisan** (the Reputation framework), and the *same act earns
opposite reactions*. A worked illustration of divergence:

| Player act | Church | Hunters | Ashbourne | Drowned | Silent | Cult | Crownsworn |
|------------|--------|---------|-----------|---------|--------|------|------------|
| Saved a village from the corrupted | ↑ trust | ↑ respect | ↑ (order) | – | ↑ | ↓ (denied them) | ↑ |
| Spared a corrupted NPC | ↓ | ↓↓ | ↓ | – | ↓ | ↑↑ | ↓ |
| Uncovered the Church's cover-up | ↓↓ | – | – (leverage) | – | ↑ | ↑ | ↓ |
| Rose in corruption stage | ↓↓ fear | ↓↓ | ↓ fear | – | ↓ | ↑↑ welcome | ↓↓ |

**Corruption is the great cross-cutting axis** (§16): it overrides ordinary reputation — at high
stages the Cult opens doors shut to all others, while the Church, Crownsworn, Hunters, and Silent
Clergy turn from wary to hostile. **There is no act that pleases everyone**; standing with one is
standing against another.

## 15. Faction Warfare (the world fights without you)

Factions **pursue goals and war whether or not the player is present** (the living-world faction
simulation, `LivingWorld.md` §17): **territorial conflict, political influence, economic warfare
(grain, roads, river-tolls), religious conflict, assassination campaigns, trade disruption, and
recruitment.** Each faction holds a **regional influence** value that rises and falls; the map of
power **visibly shifts over a playthrough** — a region can fall to the Cult, a trade war can
starve a settlement, a house can be assassinated into collapse — all from the simulation, then
*dramatized* as the player encounters it. The player is **one force among several**, not the only
mover; ignoring the politics is itself a choice with consequences.

---

## 16. Corruption & Politics

The Hollow Crown is the axis every faction is defined against. The **five stances** toward it
*are* the political fault line:

| Stance | Factions | They believe |
|--------|----------|--------------|
| **Destroy it** | Church, Crownsworn, Hunters | It is the Dusk/enemy; it must be ended |
| **Control it** | (factions/leaders who take fragments — Vane, ambitious Houses) | It is power that can be mastered (it can't) |
| **Worship it** | The Crown-Touched Cult | It is salvation/becoming |
| **Profit from it** | House Carrow and hedging Houses | It is leverage and opportunity |
| **Deny it** | Parts of the Church and Houses (publicly) | Admitting it would end order/hope |

The player's **own corruption** forces every faction to *re-read* them through this axis (§14) —
making the player not a neutral arbiter but a *political object*, courted or feared for what they
are becoming.

## 17. Player Involvement

The player can **support, betray, manipulate, lead, destroy, or unite** factions — and the
consequences are **massive and permanent** (routed through the World-State; framed as Faction
Threads, `QuestDesign.md` §12):

- **Support / Lead** — earn a faction's trust, rise within it, even come to lead it (and bend its
  goals) — at the cost of its enemies becoming yours.
- **Betray** — turn on an ally for advantage; a lasting scar that follows the player by rumor.
- **Manipulate** — play factions against each other (espionage, propaganda, brokered conflicts).
- **Destroy / Unite** — break a faction (assassinate its leaders, sever its resources) or attempt
  the near-impossible: *unite* incompatible factions against the Crown — which requires
  compromises that betray each faction's core belief.

**Faction outcomes determine:** which settlements survive and how (`LivingWorld.md`), trade and
the economy, **which NPCs live or die**, the *availability and shape of quests*, even **boss
encounters** (a faction's corrupted patron-Lord may be reachable only with that faction's help —
or be the price of betraying them), and ultimately the **endings** (§18).

---

## 18. Multiple Endings

Political and religious outcomes — together with the player's **corruption stage** and how the
**Twelve Lords / the Crown** were handled — determine the ending. The governing law:
**no ending is perfect; every ending requires sacrifice.** The ending is not a reward screen but
the world's final state, and the player must *choose what to lose.*

The **ending axes** (which combine, not a fixed menu — the finale/the King resolves them,
`BossBible.md` §15):

- **The Crown:** destroyed (and with it, perhaps, any hope of the sun's return), claimed (the
  player becomes the new Lord — power for humanity), or hidden/denied (the Dusk continues).
- **Who inherits Aldermark:** the Church's endurance, the Crownsworn's doomed restoration, an
  Ashbourne monarchy, the Cult's "becoming," the Drowned's submersion, or a fragile player-forged
  union — each *costs* the others.
- **What the player became:** human to the end, or the thing the Crown makes — which *gates*
  which endings are even reachable (a fully corrupted Crownless cannot have the Church's ending;
  the Cult's apotheosis is closed to the pure).

Sample sacrifices (illustrative, not exhaustive): *destroy the Crown* and the sky may stay dark
forever; *claim it* and save the kingdom by damning yourself; *restore the throne* and preserve a
lie that will rot again; *let the Cult win* and the world "becomes" — beautifully, monstrously.
**The game never tells the player which was right.**

---

## 19. Honesty, Production & Phasing

- **Scope reality:** a fully simulated political world of this depth is years of work; this bible
  is the **target and framework.** It defines the *fiction and the rules*; the simulation depth
  scales per the roadmap.
- **Phasing (maps to the roadmap):** the **vertical slice realizes one region's politics** — the
  Weeping Moors / Ashbourne edge — with **two or three factions in live tension** (e.g. Church,
  Ashbourne Houses, and a Cult cell), reputation that matters, one faction Thread with branching
  consequence, and a glimpse of off-screen faction movement. Prove that *moral ambiguity and
  consequence* land before scaling to all seven factions and full faction warfare.
- **The risk to watch:** factions reading as "teams" rather than beliefs. Mitigation: enforce the
  grey rule (§1) and the Public-Reputation-vs-Actual-Reality gap (§9) on every faction, and ensure
  the player meets *sympathetic, intelligent* members of factions they oppose.

---

## 20. Guardrails

- **No good faction, no evil faction** — every faction passes the grey rule (§1) or it is
  redesigned.
- **Every faction believes it is right**, has real strengths, real flaws, and a buried sin.
- **Conflict from incompatible goals**, never cartoon villainy.
- **Information is always partisan** — no faction (and no narrator) is a neutral source of truth;
  the player never gets certainty.
- **No act pleases everyone** — every allegiance costs another (§14).
- **No perfect ending** — every ending demands a sacrifice the player chooses (§18).
- **Public reputation ≠ actual reality** for every faction — the gap is the game.

> The player should finish Aldermark unable to say which faction deserved to inherit it — and
> should still be arguing, with themselves and with others, about the side they chose, years
> later, with no answer ever offered.
